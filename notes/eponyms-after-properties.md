# Describe the property before giving the eponym

Historical names are useful for credit and for finding the literature. They should not be allowed to carry the explanation.

Bad order:

> the Runge–Lenz vector is conserved.

Better order:

> the Coulomb/Kepler problem has an additional conserved vector, beyond angular momentum, pointing along the orbit's major-axis direction; this is usually called the Laplace–Runge–Lenz vector.

Bad order:

> this is a Sturm–Liouville problem.

Better order:

> this is a second-order boundary-value spectral problem whose admissible modes are orthogonal under a natural weighted inner product; this family of problems was developed by Sturm and Liouville.

The same rule applies to Hamiltonian, Lagrangian, Legendre, Laguerre, Noether, and other names.

## House rule

1. Say what the object does or what problem it answers.
2. Say why it is appearing here.
3. Give the historical name and credit.
4. Use the name as shorthand afterward.

Credit matters. Comprehension matters first.
