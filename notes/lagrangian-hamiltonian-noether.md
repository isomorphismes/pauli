# Lagrangian, Hamiltonian, and Noether without teleology

The two names that commonly organize classical mechanics are **Lagrangian** and **Hamiltonian** mechanics.

Neither should be introduced as a philosophy in which the universe is trying to optimize something.

## Lagrangian description

The Lagrangian description works with configurations and their velocities.

One constructs an **action** from an entire possible history. Physical motion is characterized by the action being **stationary** under small changes of that history.

"Stationary" is the important word. It does not generally mean globally smallest, and nothing in the statement says that nature is trying to achieve a goal.

The variational formulation is useful because it exposes symmetry cleanly.

## Noether's theorem

If a continuous transformation leaves the action unchanged, the equations carry a corresponding conserved quantity.

Examples include:

- time-translation symmetry and energy conservation;
- spatial-translation symmetry and momentum conservation;
- rotational symmetry and angular-momentum conservation.

The conserved quantity is therefore not an arbitrary constant discovered after solving the equations. It records a symmetry of the problem.

## Hamiltonian description

The Hamiltonian description reorganizes mechanics on **phase space**: configurations together with their conjugate momenta.

The Hamiltonian is the function that generates time evolution through the symplectic structure.

In ordinary conservative examples its numerical value is often total energy, which is why "Hamiltonian" and "energy" are easily conflated. Structurally the more useful statement is:

> the Hamiltonian generates time evolution in the Hamiltonian formulation.

In quantum mechanics the corresponding self-adjoint operator generates unitary time evolution.

That is not an instruction to minimize energy. A Hamiltonian trajectory may remain on a constant-energy surface while the Hamiltonian determines how the state moves along it.

## Why both descriptions exist

Lagrangian and Hamiltonian mechanics are not rival beliefs about what nature "really wants."

They are related mathematical descriptions that foreground different structures:

- Lagrangian mechanics: configurations, velocities, histories, variational structure;
- Hamiltonian mechanics: phase space, symplectic structure, generated time evolution.

Noether's theorem exposes the symmetry content of the variational description and connects naturally to conserved generators in the Hamiltonian description.
