# Bound and scattering states

Do not say "to get a bound state" as though a bound state were a desired aesthetic outcome.

The question is:

> what kinds of states does this operator actually admit?

For the Coulomb problem there are two qualitatively different spectral regimes.

## Bound states

A bound energy eigenstate is localized around the attractive center and is an actual finite-norm eigenvector in the Hilbert space.

For hydrogen, these occur at discrete negative energies when zero energy is chosen as the separated electron-and-proton threshold.

The discreteness is a result of the spectral problem and its boundary behavior. It is not inserted by requesting that the electron be bound.

## Scattering states

Positive-energy solutions describe the continuum in which an electron can come from far away, interact with the Coulomb center, and return to large distance.

The formal energy eigenfunctions used to describe this continuum are generally not square-integrable vectors themselves. Physical scattering states are finite-norm wave packets assembled from the continuum.

## "Bound" is relative to an interaction

An electron can be unbound from any particular proton while the plasma containing it remains gravitationally bound to a star.

Always say what system and what interaction define the word "bound."
