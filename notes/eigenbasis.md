# Eigenbasis

An eigenfunction is not a special smooth analogue of an eigenvector. It **is an eigenvector** when the vector space happens to consist of functions.

An operator has an eigenvector when acting on that nonzero vector only rescales it.

An **eigenbasis** is a basis made from eigenvectors of the operator.

For a function space, the basis elements are functions, so one often says "basis functions" or "eigenfunctions."

## Spherical harmonics

The spherical Laplacian acts on functions on the sphere.

Its eigenspaces are the spherical-harmonic spaces. Taken together, spherical harmonics provide an orthogonal basis for square-integrable functions on the sphere.

That means an angular function can be decomposed into its spherical-harmonic components just as a finite-dimensional vector can be decomposed into components along basis directions.

## Infinite-dimensional warning

Not every operator has a basis of ordinary eigenvectors.

Continuous spectrum requires a more general spectral decomposition. Plane waves and Coulomb scattering modes are standard examples where physicists use generalized eigenfunctions that are not themselves finite-norm vectors in the Hilbert space.

So "spectral decomposition" is the general idea. "Eigenbasis" is an important special case.
