# Coulomb geometry before the formula

The inverse-square electric field should not first appear as a formula to memorize.

In three-dimensional space, the set of points at a fixed distance from a point source is a sphere. Its area grows as the square of that distance.

If a conserved radial flux passes through every surrounding sphere, the same total amount is spread across larger and larger area. Therefore the field strength falls as inverse distance squared.

The Coulomb **potential** falls as inverse distance because the field is the spatial derivative of the potential.

## Dimensional statement

In a space with (d) spatial dimensions, the area of a sphere grows like distance to the power (d-1). A conserved radial field therefore falls like the reciprocal of that power.

Integrating radially gives the corresponding potential. Three dimensions are special in giving the familiar reciprocal-distance potential.

## Why this matters for hydrogen

The Coulomb potential, spherical geometry, rotational symmetry, spherical harmonics, and the radial/angular decomposition are not unrelated formulas.

They are different manifestations of the geometry of a point source in three-dimensional space.

The coordinate formulas should be downstream from that observation.
