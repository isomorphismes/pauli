# Structure before integer labels

Do not organize the exposition around "quantum numbers."

The integers used in standard hydrogen notation are useful labels for structures that should already have been identified. Presenting the labels first makes the bookkeeping look fundamental.

For the angular part of hydrogen, begin with:

> rotations act on the space of angular states.

Then decompose that representation into irreducible rotation types.

Only after that decomposition, and only after choosing an axis and a basis adapted to that axis, do integer labels become useful.

The integers do not control the universe. They record where a state sits in a decomposition that came from symmetry.

Likewise, the discrete energy labels for bound hydrogen should appear after the spectral problem has been explained.

## House rule

Do not introduce an integer label unless the reader already knows:

- what object is being classified;
- what equivalence or symmetry makes the classification natural;
- what changing the label changes;
- what does not depend on the chosen coordinates or basis.

If the code needs short names such as `l` or `m`, that is an implementation concern. The explanatory notes should foreground the structure.
