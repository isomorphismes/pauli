# Don S. Lemons — *Thermodynamic Weirdness*

Don S. Lemons, *Thermodynamic Weirdness: From Fahrenheit to Clausius*, MIT Press, 2020.

Publisher page:

https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262538947/thermodynamic-weirdness/

Keep this book nearby for one narrow but useful reason: familiar numerical scales were not handed down from nature with their coordinate systems already chosen.

The book follows temperature, heat, engines, energy, and entropy in an order that is both historical and conceptual. Fahrenheit, caloric theories, Carnot, Kelvin, Joule, and Clausius appear because each stage solves a real problem left by the previous one.

For `pauli`, the most useful small lesson is the thermometer story.

A scale is a representation of a physical ordering and comparison procedure. Changing units can be linear when the origin is preserved, or affine when an offset is introduced. That is a concrete route into the otherwise abstract distinction between linear and affine transformations.

This is not a primary hydrogen source.

It is a source to reach for when a short physical story can explain why a mathematical distinction exists before notation is introduced.
