# David Hestenes — Oersted Medal Lecture: *Reforming the Mathematical Language of Physics*

David Hestenes, Oersted Medal Lecture 2002, published in *American Journal of Physics* 71(2), 2003, pp. 104–121.

Overview and links:

https://davidhestenes.net/geocalc/html/Overview.html

This should be difficult to miss in `pauli`.

Hestenes' central complaint is that physics is taught using a fragmented collection of mathematical languages. That fragmentation is not harmless: the language available to the student controls which structures are easy to see and which are buried under translation rules.

## The wedge product belongs up front

The wedge product

```text
a ∧ b
```

represents oriented area directly.

Physics is full of oriented planes, fluxes, rotations, angular momentum, differential forms, and higher-dimensional analogues. Replacing an oriented area by a special three-dimensional pseudovector may be convenient in one setting, but it hides what kind of object is actually present and stops generalizing cleanly.

This is a central pedagogical warning for `pauli`:

> do not withhold the wedge product and then spend years teaching dimension-specific tricks whose geometric meaning the wedge product would have made explicit.

The point is not merely elegance. Mathematical language changes what one can recognize.

## Relation to Burke

Burke attacks much of the same problem through differential forms. Hestenes approaches it through geometric algebra. The formal systems are not identical, but the shared lesson is important:

> teach the geometric object, not only the conventional notation inherited from three-dimensional vector calculus.

That lesson should remain visible in the orbital application, not hidden at the bottom of a bibliography.
