# Emmy Noether

Emmy Noether belongs in the main references even though no single book is designated here.

Two parts of her work are directly relevant to `pauli`.

## Symmetry and conservation

Her 1918 work on invariant variational problems gives the structural relation between continuous symmetries and conservation laws.

Do not say that a system "tries to minimize energy" or "wants to minimize action." Those phrases make a mathematical characterization sound like willpower.

A variational formulation specifies a functional on possible histories. Physical trajectories satisfy a **stationarity** condition. Continuous transformations preserving that variational structure lead to conserved quantities.

That is a statement about the structure and symmetry of the space of possible histories, not about nature pursuing a goal.

Examples that matter throughout mechanics are:

- invariance under shifting time;
- invariance under shifting position;
- invariance under rotating the entire configuration.

The associated conserved quantities are consequences of those symmetries, not arbitrary constants discovered after the equations have been solved.

## Algebra

Noether's importance is much larger than the theorem bearing her name. Her work helped transform algebra toward the structural study of rings, ideals, modules, and finiteness conditions.

That broader achievement is stylistically relevant: identify the structure organizing many calculations rather than treating each calculation as an isolated trick.
