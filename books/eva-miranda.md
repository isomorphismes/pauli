# Eva Miranda

Eva Miranda is a reference for the geometric side of Hamiltonian mechanics: symplectic and Poisson geometry, integrable systems, group actions, singularities, and geometric quantization.

Research page:

https://web.mat.upc.edu/eva.miranda/nova/

The useful habit for `pauli` is to treat Hamiltonian mechanics as geometry before it becomes a bag of formulas. Start with the phase space and the structure carried by it; then explain symmetries, conserved quantities, integrability, and quantization.

## *Geometry and Dynamics of Integrable Systems*

Edited by Eva Miranda and Vladimir Matveev, Springer, 2016.

https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-319-33503-2

The volume brings together differential Galois theory, singularities of bi-Hamiltonian systems, Poisson pencils, stability, and the geometry of integrable systems.

For this repository the important point is not to make “integrable” mean merely “we found a closed formula.” Integrability has geometric structure, and singular systems are part of the story rather than an annoying exception.

## *On symplectic linearization of singular Lagrangian foliations*

Miranda's doctoral thesis, Universitat de Barcelona, 2003.

https://hdl.handle.net/10803/678

This belongs nearby whenever `pauli` discusses normal forms or local models. A complicated Hamiltonian system can often be understood by asking which part of its geometry can be replaced, near a singularity, by a simpler model without destroying the structure that matters.

## Geometric quantization of integrable systems with hyperbolic singularities

Mark Hamilton and Eva Miranda, *Annales de l'Institut Fourier* 60(1), 2010, 51–85.

https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.0338

This is a useful bridge between classical phase-space geometry and quantum structure. The polarization comes from an integrable system, and its singularities affect the quantization.

That is exactly the sort of bridge `pauli` should keep visible: quantum formulas do not arrive from nowhere. They are tied to choices and structures already present in the classical geometry.

## Standing use in this repository

Miranda is here to keep several distinctions visible:

- Hamiltonian mechanics is not synonymous with writing down a Hamiltonian function.
- Symplectic and Poisson structure should be explained before coordinate calculations obscure them.
- Symmetry and group actions belong naturally to mechanics.
- Singularities are geometric objects to understand, not merely places where a formula stops working.
- Quantization should be presented as a relation between classical and quantum structures, not as a replacement of one mysterious rulebook by another.
