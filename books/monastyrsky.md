# Michael Monastyrsky — *Riemann, Topology, and Physics*

Michael Monastyrsky, *Riemann, Topology, and Physics*.

This belongs in the main books section because it models a way of talking about mathematics and physics in which a mathematical structure has a history, a reason for being introduced, and physical lives beyond one narrow application.

The book combines a short account of Riemann with a survey of topological ideas in physics, including spontaneous symmetry breaking, gauge fields, topological particles, solitons, and knots.

## Why it matters here

The useful lesson for `pauli` is not one particular theorem but the explanatory order:

- geometry is not decoration on top of formulas;
- topology is not machinery added after the real calculation;
- one mathematical structure may organize several apparently different physical phenomena;
- history can clarify why a concept was needed without reducing the mathematics to biography.

It is also a useful corrective to the idea that gauge theory belongs specifically to quantum field theory. Connections, bundles, holonomy, curvature, and symmetry belong to geometry and classical mechanics as well.

When a physical calculation in `pauli` can be understood as a manifestation of geometry or topology, say that first. The coordinate formula comes later.
