# Jeremy Gray — *Change and Variations*

Full title: *Change and Variations: A History of Differential Equations to 1900*.

Gray is useful here less as a source of formulas than as a guide to **why differential equations took the forms they did** and what problems made mathematicians care about them.

For `laguerre`, use Gray's historical style when introducing:

- ordinary versus partial differential equations,
- heat, wave, and potential problems,
- separation of variables,
- the changing meaning of "solution",
- boundary and initial conditions,
- the emergence of spectral and eigenfunction methods.

The desired explanatory order is historical and structural:

1. what physical or mathematical problem was being studied;
2. what kind of differential equation arose;
3. what obstacle existing methods ran into;
4. what new concept or method resolved it;
5. only then the modern compact notation.

This is especially useful whenever a named equation or special function otherwise appears as if from nowhere.

Gray explicitly does not make Sturm–Liouville theory a major subject of *Change and Variations* because Jesper Lützen's historical treatment of Liouville covers that development in much greater depth. See [Jesper Lützen](jesper-lutzen.md).
