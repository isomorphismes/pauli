# Warning: do not use Jackson as the expository model

Reference: J. D. Jackson, *Classical Electrodynamics*.

This is **not** a claim that Jackson is a bad book. It is a warning about what `laguerre` should not imitate.

Jackson is useful when the task is to become effective at classical-electrodynamics calculations and boundary-value techniques. That is not the explanatory order wanted here.

Do not present a mathematical object by:

1. choosing coordinates,
2. separating variables,
3. manipulating the resulting ordinary differential equations,
4. naming whatever special functions appear,
5. and treating successful calculation as though it explained why those objects were natural.

For this project, symmetry and structure come first.

For example, spherical harmonics should first appear as the irreducible pieces of the rotation action on functions on the sphere, equivalently as eigenspaces of the rotation-invariant spherical Laplacian. Associated Legendre formulas come later, after an axis and coordinates have been chosen.

Use Jackson as a source of computations when useful. Do not use it as the model for the conceptual architecture of the notes.
