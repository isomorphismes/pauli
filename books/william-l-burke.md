# William Lionel Burke

William L. Burke is a primary style reference for `pauli`, even when the particular subject is not quantum mechanics.

His recurring strength is to identify the geometric object first, take definitions seriously, and then connect the geometry to physical examples.

Burke's UCSC/Lick pages are also worth preserving as a source index:

https://www.ucolick.org/~burke/home.html

## *Applied Differential Geometry*

Cambridge University Press, 1985.

This is a self-contained introduction to differential geometry and differential forms written primarily for physicists. Its chapters move through linear and affine structure, manifolds, transformations, differential forms and exterior calculus, electrodynamics, particle and field dynamics, fiber bundles, and gravitation.

The important stylistic point is that the geometric objects are not introduced as fancy replacements for a completed vector-calculus treatment. The geometry explains what the calculations are actually about.

Burke's treatment of transformations is especially relevant to `pauli`: a mathematical structure can often be understood through the transformations that preserve it. That is exactly the attitude needed when rotations and their representations organize the angular part of hydrogen.

## *Spacetime, Geometry, Cosmology*

Burke also wrote *Spacetime, Geometry, Cosmology*, which his own differential-forms reference page describes as a casual introduction with applications reaching into cosmology and water waves.

Reference page:

https://www.ucolick.org/~burke/forms/books.html

Keep it here because it reinforces the broader point: the same geometric language should move between mechanics, fields, spacetime, waves, and cosmology. Geometry is not a special-purpose dialect for one theory.

## *Div, Grad, Curl are Dead*

Burke's unfinished draft:

https://www.ucolick.org/~burke/forms/dgcad.html

The table of contents moves from linear and affine structure through 1-forms, differential forms, pullbacks, exterior derivatives, Stokes' theorem, symmetry and conservation, electrodynamics, Hamiltonian and Lagrangian mechanics, and waves.

The title states an important rule for this repository. Familiar three-dimensional notation can hide the actual geometric distinctions between objects. Do not let an accidental notation dictate the conceptual architecture.

In particular, the wedge product retains oriented area and higher-dimensional structure directly. A three-dimensional cross-product alias may be convenient, but it should not be allowed to hide the underlying object.

## Standing use in this repository

Burke is not here because every page pertains to the hydrogen atom. He is here because the style is right:

> identify the object, preserve its geometry, and let coordinates serve the object rather than define it.
