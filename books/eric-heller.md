# Eric J. Heller — *Why You Hear What You Hear*

Eric J. Heller, *Why You Hear What You Hear: An Experiential Approach to Sound, Music, and Psychoacoustics*, Princeton University Press.

Author/book resources:

https://www-heller.harvard.edu/why-you-hear-what-you-hear

https://www.whyyouhearwhatyouhear.com/

This belongs in `pauli` for the same reason the temperature books do: it gives us familiar physical experiences to reach for before introducing abstract machinery.

Heller builds the physics of sound around things a reader can actually hear.

For this project, the useful bridges include:

- two instruments playing the same pitch but sounding different;
- timbre as more than one number;
- resonances and normal modes;
- spectra and time envelopes;
- the physical production of sound and the perceptual result.

The companion material explicitly includes instrument-recognition examples where flute, oboe, clarinet, and trumpet can be compared by timbre.

That makes it a good source when the word *mode*, *spectrum*, or *eigenfunction* threatens to become detached from ordinary experience.

The point is not to turn the hydrogen notebook into an acoustics course.

The point is that a reader already knows, with their ears, that one nominal pitch can be realized by many different structured patterns.
