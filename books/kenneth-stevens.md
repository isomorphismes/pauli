# Kenneth N. Stevens — *Acoustic Phonetics*

Kenneth N. Stevens, *Acoustic Phonetics*, MIT Press.

Publisher page:

https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262692502/acoustic-phonetics/

This is a serious reference for the speech side of the sound analogy.

Stevens develops speech sound from the physical production system:

- source mechanisms;
- vocal-tract resonators;
- auditory processing;
- vowels;
- consonants;
- context.

For `pauli`, the useful familiar example is the vowel.

A person can hold roughly the same vocal-fold pitch while changing the shape of the vocal tract and thereby changing which resonances are emphasized. The perceived vowel changes.

That makes a concrete bridge to the general idea:

> geometry and boundary conditions select which modes are favored.

Do not imply that speech acoustics and hydrogen orbitals are the same mathematical problem.

Use the analogy only where it is doing real explanatory work: shape, resonance, modes, and the fact that a complicated pattern can be organized into a small number of natural components.
