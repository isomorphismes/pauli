# Predrag Cvitanović

Predrag Cvitanović belongs in the main `pauli` references for three overlapping reasons:

1. **Birdtracks** — exact diagrammatic reasoning in group and representation theory.
2. **ChaosBook** — an unusually personal, funny, geometrically motivated treatment of nonlinear dynamics, periodic-orbit theory, and quantum chaos.
3. **Quefithe** — one of the best short fables about two mathematically different descriptions of the same physics.

## *Group Theory: Birdtracks, Lie's, and Exceptional Groups*

Princeton University Press, 2008.

Stable webbook / project site:

https://birdtracks.eu/

Stable PDF:

https://birdtracks.eu/version9.0/GroupTheory.pdf

Table of contents:

https://birdtracks.eu/TOC.html

The book contains thousands of diagrams and systematically develops diagrammatic projection operators, invariant tensors, Lie groups, spinors, and exceptional groups.

The chapter route includes:

- invariants and reducibility;
- diagrammatic notation;
- recouplings;
- permutations;
- Casimir operators;
- group integrals;
- unitary, orthogonal, symplectic, and spinor groups;
- negative dimensions;
- exceptional groups and exceptional magic.

## Why Birdtracks matters to `pauli`

Birdtracks make tensor contractions and invariant structure visible.

An indexed expression may require the reader to remember which repeated symbol is connected to which tensor. A birdtrack draws the connection.

That is not merely prettier notation. It changes what can be noticed.

For `pauli`, Birdtracks is a standing reminder:

> when connectivity is the mathematics, draw the connectivity.

This is especially useful for:

- projection operators;
- decomposition of tensor products;
- invariant tensors;
- angular-momentum coupling;
- traces;
- group averaging;
- recoupling identities.

The diagrams should remain exact enough that one can translate them back into algebra when necessary.

## Relation to the spiders book

See [Coecke and Kissinger](coecke-kissinger.md).

The two traditions should remain distinct but adjacent:

- Birdtracks: representation theory, invariant tensors, projectors.
- Spider/string diagrams: compositional quantum processes and tensor structure.

Both show that indexed algebra is not the only serious notation available.

## *Chaos: Classical and Quantum*

Current webbook:

https://chaosbook.org/chapters/ChaosBook.pdf

Main site:

https://chaosbook.org/

Cvitanović and collaborators organize chaotic dynamics around geometry, periodic orbits, symbolic dynamics, trace formulas, determinants, and cycle expansions.

The book is valuable here even when the particular topic is irrelevant to hydrogen.

The **style** is worth preserving:

- start from a geometric picture or physical problem;
- make the difficult idea concrete;
- permit jokes and strange chapter titles when they actually help memory;
- return to intuition after a run of formal derivation;
- distinguish what can be calculated from what the calculation means;
- do not sound like an institution wrote the prose.

A chapter called “Why cycle?” is allowed to slow down after pages of formulas and ask what the formulas are actually saying.

That is a much healthier instinct than moving directly to the next formalism.

The Week 12 material develops cycle expansions, pseudo-cycles, shadowing, cycle formulas for averages, and the way short cycles can encode long-time behavior.

Whether or not cycle expansions ever become part of `pauli`, keep this link.

It is a good example of Cvitanović's best explanatory habit: a complicated formal expansion is reorganized around a geometric fact one can picture — long periodic orbits are shadowed by combinations of shorter ones.

The subject is not being linked because `pauli` needs a chapter on chaotic dynamics. It is being linked because the exposition is worth returning to.

## *Field Theory* and the Quefithe fable

Field-theory material:

https://chaosbook.org/FieldTheory/

The fable:

https://chaosbook.org/FieldTheory/quefithe.html

Character key:

https://chaosbook.org/FieldTheory/quefitheKey.html

The fable is about two travelers describing the land of **Quefithe**, meaning quantum field theory.

The characters are:

- **mole — Julian Schwinger**
- **crow — Richard Feynman**
- **wise owl — Niels Bohr / the older quantum generation**
- **frogs — mathematicians**
- **fox — Freeman Dyson**
- **snails — Cvitanović and the rest of us**

The mole experiences Quefithe as tunnels splitting and rejoining: the Schwinger-style formal route.

The crow sees mountains, passes, valleys, and little moles running below: Feynman's visual/diagrammatic route.

The crow is the one said to sound as though he had taken too many bubble baths.

The fox keeps running between the two descriptions until he understands that they describe the same place.

## Why Quefithe belongs in this repository

It says something serious in the form of a children's story:

> two mathematical descriptions can look nothing alike and still describe the same physical structure.

That is directly relevant to `pauli`.

Examples:

- differential equations versus representation theory;
- matrix coordinates versus abstract linear maps;
- special functions versus symmetry spaces;
- tensor indices versus diagrams;
- classical Kepler geometry versus quantum SO(4);
- chemistry orbital language versus mathematical representation language.

The job is not to declare one description the only legitimate one.

The job is to understand the translations well enough to move between them like Dyson's fox.

## Cvitanović style rules

These are project rules, not quotations.

### Explain the strange thing plainly

If the mathematics has a surprising geometric picture, lead with the picture.

### Permit personality

Technical prose does not become more rigorous by sounding bureaucratic.

A joke, fable, odd chapter name, or historical aside is welcome when it makes the structure easier to remember.

### Come back after the derivation

After a long calculation, ask what was learned.

A derivation that never returns to the object has not finished explaining it.

### Use pictures as mathematics

Birdtracks, orbit diagrams, symbolic dynamics, and shadowing pictures all carry actual structure.

Do not relegate them to decoration.

### Respect multiple descriptions

When two formalisms describe the same thing, make the translation itself part of the subject.
