# Chris Quigg — *Colloquium: A Century of Noether's Theorem*

Chris Quigg, 2019.

Preprint:

https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.01989

The essay grew from a Fermilab colloquium given in August 2018 for the centenary of Emmy Noether's theorem.

## Why it belongs here

This piece keeps together three things that should not be separated:

- Emmy Noether's mathematical work;
- the historical setting in which her theorem appeared;
- the physical meaning of the relation between continuous symmetry and conservation law.

The useful question is:

> what transformations leave the physical problem unchanged, and what quantities remain constant because of that invariance?

That is more informative than memorizing a table saying that time translation "gives energy" and rotation "gives angular momentum."

Quigg also places the theorem beside Noether's larger algebraic achievement. That is appropriate for this repository: the theorem is not an isolated physics trick attached to a famous name.

Use the historical name after the property has been stated clearly.
