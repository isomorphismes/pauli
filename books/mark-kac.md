# Mark Kac — *Can One Hear the Shape of a Drum?*

Mark Kac, “Can One Hear the Shape of a Drum?”, *American Mathematical Monthly* 73 (1966), 1–23.

A readable copy is available here:

https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~saito/courses/ACHA.READ.F03/kac-drum.pdf

Publisher record:

https://doi.org/10.1080/00029890.1966.11970915

The title is the reason to keep this reference close.

A membrane of a particular shape has a particular collection of natural vibration frequencies.

Kac asks whether the complete collection of those frequencies determines the shape itself.

That is a memorable way to encounter the spectrum of the Laplacian without beginning with either of those technical words.

For `pauli`, the useful conceptual chain is:

```text
shape
→ local vibration rule
→ natural modes
→ natural frequencies
→ what can the collection of frequencies tell us about the shape?
```

This is also a useful warning about exposition.

One can say that Kac asked the question and helped make it famous without turning “Kac” into another label the reader is expected to memorize.

The question and the structure matter more than the surname.
