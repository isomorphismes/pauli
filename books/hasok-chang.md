# Hasok Chang — *Inventing Temperature*

Hasok Chang, *Inventing Temperature: Measurement and Scientific Progress*, Oxford University Press, 2004.

Publisher page:

https://academic.oup.com/book/5530

Chang asks a deceptively basic question:

> What is temperature, and how could anyone know that a thermometer measures it correctly?

The history matters because early thermometry had a circular problem. Different substances expanded differently. Fixed points had to be chosen. Instruments disagreed. Scientists needed standards for deciding which regularities belonged to temperature itself and which belonged to the material used to measure it.

The book is useful for `pauli` because it shows how a mathematical representation becomes trustworthy.

A coordinate or scale is not the physical quantity itself.
A successful representation preserves relations that experiments and theory give us reason to care about.

That is a useful story to keep beside linear algebra:

- inches and centimeters can share an origin and differ by a linear rescaling;
- Celsius and Fahrenheit also include an offset and are therefore affine;
- deciding what counts as a meaningful zero is a physical question before it is an algebraic one.

Chang is not here as a hydrogen reference.

He is here because he makes a basic mathematical distinction feel necessary rather than ceremonial.
