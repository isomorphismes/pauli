# N. M. J. Woodhouse — *Geometric Quantization*

Oxford University Press:

https://academic.oup.com/book/54006

Geometric quantization asks for a geometric account of the passage from classical Hamiltonian systems to quantum systems. The program is associated especially with Kostant and Souriau.

## Why the machine suggested it

Ordinary physics texts often introduce "quantization" through replacement rules: take a classical quantity and replace it by an operator satisfying certain commutation relations.

That can be computationally useful while leaving the deeper question unanswered:

> what mathematical structure is being carried from classical mechanics into quantum mechanics, and what obstructs or constrains that passage?

Woodhouse is relevant because it starts from symplectic/Hamiltonian geometry and treats quantization as a geometric construction rather than as a magic dictionary.

Topics around which it may be useful include:

- symplectic manifolds;
- Hamiltonian flows;
- line bundles and connections;
- polarizations;
- symmetry in quantization.
