# George W. Mackey — *Mathematical Foundations of Quantum Mechanics*

Originally based on Mackey's Harvard lectures; later reprinted by Dover.

Google Books bibliographic page:

https://books.google.com/books?id=Gcsz0c39QzgC

The publisher description emphasizes generality and careful formulation rather than problem-solving.

## Why it is unusually relevant

Mackey moves from classical mechanics and phase space into quantum dynamics, Hilbert-space structure, self-adjoint operators, one-parameter unitary groups, and then explicitly into **group theory and the quantum mechanics of the atom**.

The final material includes representation theory, spherical symmetry, spin, classification of eigenvalues by symmetry, and the many-electron atom.

That makes the book especially relevant to the direction of `pauli`:

> symmetry and representation theory should explain the organization of states before coordinate formulas and integer labels are allowed to dominate the story.

## What to consult

Use Mackey when a question connects:

- symmetry groups to state spaces;
- unitary representations to quantum transformations;
- time evolution to one-parameter groups;
- spherical symmetry to atomic state structure;
- spectral decomposition to physical classification.

This may be a particularly good source for keeping "representation" physical: the representation is how a symmetry acts on the state space, not a fancy synonym for a matrix.
