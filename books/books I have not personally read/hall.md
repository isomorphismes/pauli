# Brian C. Hall — *Quantum Theory for Mathematicians*

Author page:

https://sites.nd.edu/brian-hall/quantum-theory-for-mathematicians/

Hall describes the book as a self-contained treatment of quantum mechanics, excluding quantum field theory, from a mathematical perspective. It begins with the experiments that motivated quantum mechanics rather than assuming the formalism from the start.

## Why the machine suggested it

This is close to the immediate needs of `pauli`: ordinary quantum mechanics, but written for readers who are willing to think in Hilbert spaces, operators, Lie groups, Lie algebras, and representations.

The topics for which it is especially worth consulting are:

- the mathematical formulation of states and observables;
- unitary time evolution and self-adjoint generators;
- angular momentum and spin through group/representation structure;
- the hydrogen atom;
- the larger symmetry of the Coulomb problem;
- the relation between classical mechanics and quantization.

## What to mine from it

For the hydrogen notes, use Hall as a check against explanations that begin with integer labels or separation formulas.

The desired question is:

> what symmetry is acting, what space is it acting on, and how does that space decompose?

Only after that should the standard coordinate expressions appear.

Hall may also be useful when the informal phrase "the Hamiltonian is self-adjoint" needs to be unpacked into an operator with a domain rather than treated as a formal differential expression.
