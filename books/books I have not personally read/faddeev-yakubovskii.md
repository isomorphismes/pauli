## What the book actually starts from

The opening chapters are not a rush into hydrogen formulas. They begin with:

- the algebra of observables in classical mechanics;
- states;
- Liouville's theorem;
- different pictures of motion in classical mechanics;
- the physical basis of quantum mechanics;
- a finite-dimensional model before the full infinite-dimensional theory.

Later chapters move through quantum states, uncertainty, eigenvalues and eigenvectors of observables, Schrödinger and Heisenberg pictures, coordinate and momentum representations, angular momentum, the classical limit, and standard examples.

## Why the machine suggested it

That sequence is useful for `pauli` because it asks what the basic objects and motions are before using the standard calculational machinery.

It may be especially valuable when explaining:

- why an "observable" is represented by an operator;
- why two pictures of time evolution can describe the same physics;
- why generalized eigenfunctions appear;
- how angular momentum sits inside the general theory rather than being an isolated hydrogen trick.
