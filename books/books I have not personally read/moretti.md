# Valter Moretti — *Spectral Theory and Quantum Mechanics*

Springer page:

https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-319-70706-8

The book is a mathematical treatment of quantum mechanics built around functional analysis, operator theory, spectral theory, symmetry, and an introduction to algebraic formulations.

Its early chapters develop Hilbert spaces and bounded operators before moving to densely defined unbounded operators. That order matters because the differential operators of quantum mechanics are typically unbounded.

## Why the machine suggested it

This is the reference to reach for when a physicist's formal operator notation hides an analytical issue.

Important examples for `pauli` include:

- the difference between symmetric and self-adjoint;
- why an operator's domain is part of the operator;
- how boundary conditions enter self-adjointness;
- what the spectral theorem really says;
- how discrete and continuous spectrum differ;
- why generalized eigenfunctions are needed for scattering states.

These are not technical decorations. They determine whether the formal expression actually defines the claimed quantum dynamics.

## House use

Use Moretti to prevent false statements.

Do not make the primary exposition read like a functional-analysis textbook when the analytical subtlety does not change the concept being explained.
