# Jesper Lützen — *Joseph Liouville 1809–1882*

Full title: *Joseph Liouville 1809–1882: Master of Pure and Applied Mathematics*.

This is the historical reference for the development usually compressed into the phrase **Sturm–Liouville theory**.

Use it when `laguerre` needs to explain:

- who Sturm and Liouville were;
- whether and how their work interacted;
- what differential-equation problems they were actually trying to solve;
- why eigenvalues, orthogonality, zeros, boundary conditions, and expansion in eigenfunctions emerged together;
- how the subject developed before the modern Hilbert-space and self-adjoint-operator language existed.

The point is to avoid presenting a "Sturm–Liouville problem" merely as a template equation.

The modern spectral language should be connected back to the older concrete questions: when does a boundary-value problem admit a solution, for which parameter values, how are different solutions related, and can arbitrary functions be expanded in those special solutions?

That history is directly relevant to understanding why Legendre and Laguerre families are mathematically natural rather than arbitrary tables of polynomials.
